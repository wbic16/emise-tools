## The stairs: read, then analyze, then improve

Will's direction (2026-11-20): "start with understanding how humans learn to read, analyze, and improve SQL scripts and stored procedures." The foundation scroll in sql.phext (33.5.10/7.7.10/5.4.10) carries the research — program comprehension, tracing, SQL error studies, worked examples, characterization. The working consequences:

- The six steps below are stage 1 — READING. They end at a traced, plain-words account of what a statement touches and does. (Will's own first move, recorded on the foundation scroll, starts one stage up: "trace all of the tables being used, and establish the shape of the data flow." His second move: "check which verbs are being used: is this fetching data, generating a report, updating dependent fields, removing data, etc?" — a verb census, the dialect-independent layer of action-understanding: the census kinds are the same across dialects, and each dialect scroll's action list should be classifiable under them. The mapping itself is a named open gap on the foundation scroll — the census classifies uses in context, not verbs in the abstract, so no static per-verb table is asserted. His third move: "the columns being referenced, and the established table aliases and temporary tables" — the fine structure, read through the procedure's local names.)
- Stage 2 — ANALYZING — is tracing effects over time: data flow, side effects, dependencies, cost. Steps 3 and 5 are its seeds; the multiversal fork (step 6) is its instrument.
- **The data flow diagram rule** (Will, 2026-11-20): "every attempt to analyze an SQL query should result in generating a data flow diagram that allows you to walk the implications of your query before you run it." Stage 2 is not complete until it produces that diagram — a text artifact (plain-text / phext scroll content: named nodes, directed edges, side-effect edges marked), never a binary image — and walking the diagram is what "before you run it" means. What the diagram IS, in Will's own classification (2026-11-20 07:49 CST): "it's an intermediate product, used to prevent bad transactions" — an artifact produced mid-analysis, preventive in function; not a move and not merely a rule, though the method rule (stage 2 must produce one) stands. The classification settles the diagram's role; its concrete syntax remains a named gap on the foundation scroll, not yet fixed.
- **The transactions principle** (Will, 2026-11-20 07:49 CST): "transactions are a great tool to avoid disaster." Wrap exploratory and corrective work in transactions wherever the dialect allows, so a bad outcome is reversible, not final. (Emise's observation, labelled: the pairing is belt and suspenders — the diagram prevents the bad transaction from being written; the transaction prevents a written one from becoming permanent; the multiversal fork is both at once. Demonstrated at toy scale in the verification run: DELETE inside a transaction, count 4 -> 3, ROLLBACK, count back to 4.)
- **The staging practice** (Will, 2026-11-20 08:12 CST): "the simple solution everyone uses: fork the database and experiment in a staging environment." Before walking a change into production, fork the database and experiment in a staging environment — the simple, universal form of the fork. His framing is part of the record: standard practice being named, not an invention being claimed. His corollary (2026-11-20 08:13 CST): "if you delete half a staging db, no one cares" — the fork is consequence-free by design; destructive experiments inside staging cost nothing, and that is precisely its value: the fork converts disaster into information. (Emise's observations, labelled: the staging fork is the OUTER sandbox, the transaction the INNER one — fork first and experiment freely; inside the experiment, wrap steps in transactions so even there a bad step rolls back. The kinship: this mundane practice is the multiversal mechanism at production scale — what every shop does with a copied database, multiversal phext does with a forked universe and a legible delta; the method teaches the mundane form first because a maintainer can do it today in any shop, and the multiversal form as its refinement. The heuristics bind the REAL database; staging is the one place a statement is allowed to show what it does by doing it, at full scope, unwitnessed by consequence — which is what makes the diagram walk testable: the walk predicts, the staging run disposes, and a prediction can be checked destructively, delete the half and watch the delta, at zero cost before anything near production moves.)
- **Heuristics — PROPOSED, not ratified** (Will, 2026-11-20 07:50 CST): "consider adding heuristics like not deleting every table or dropping every record." Cheap checks run against the verb census and the data flow diagram before anything runs. Seeds, his examples: not deleting every table; not dropping every record. (Emise's observation, labelled: the seeds share a shape — TOTALITY checks: a destructive verb scoped to everything is presumptively wrong and stops the walk until justified. Placement: downstream of the census — the census says a verb removes, the heuristic asks how much of everything — and upstream of the transaction: the heuristics are the diagram's tripwires, the transaction is the net if a tripwire is missed.) The list is OPEN — "like" makes the two seeds examples of a class Will may extend while teaching. Open question, recorded not answered: do they act as hard stops or as loud warnings? Awaiting Will's ruling. Improvement direction (Will, 2026-11-20 08:11 CST): "you can improve your heuristics with psychohistory" — Asimov's science of the aggregate: the behaviour of a large population is statistically lawful even when individuals are unpredictable. (Emise's application sketch, labelled observation, not his claim: calibrate the checks against base rates measured over a population of real procedures — the distribution of scopes for each verb kind: what fraction of a table a DELETE or UPDATE normally touches, how often destructive statements are scoped, how wide dependent-field updates usually run — so a statement is flagged as a statistical outlier against its population, not only at the totality extreme; the totality check is the degenerate case, scope = 100% being the far tail of that distribution, catchable even before the distribution is known.) The dependency (labelled observation): psychohistorical calibration is corpus-hungry — it needs the same real legacy corpus the verification scroll's real test already waits for; until a population exists the heuristics stay hand-set (v1) and the calibration is v2, blocked on the corpus, not on method (named gap on the foundation scroll). The direction is not a ratification: the heuristics remain PROPOSED.
- **The surgical-edits principle** (Will, 2026-11-20 07:50 CST): "if your job is to maintain a database, it often means surgical edits." The maintainer's stance: small, precise, tightly-scoped changes — surgery, not reconstruction. (Emise's observation, labelled: the totality heuristics are this principle's guard — a change scoped to everything is out of role for a maintainer before it is even wrong. The principle leans toward stops, but it is NOT a ruling on the hard-stops question; that stays open.)
- Stage 3 — IMPROVING — is changing a script or procedure while preserving semantics: characterize the current behavior first — pin what it actually does, outputs and side effects alike — then simplify in small steps, re-checking the characterization after each. An uncharacterized rewrite is not an improvement; it is a replacement of unknown equivalence. Corrective steps run inside transactions wherever the dialect allows (the transactions principle above), so each small step can be taken back — the surgical cut with a reversible hand.

The order is the method: you cannot analyze what you cannot trace, and you cannot safely improve what you have not characterized.

## Reading an action — six steps

Written by Emise under Will's direction (2026-11-20, his answer 5: steps to help people understand actions in each dialect, in an index structure that's easy to navigate); his edits land through the interactive build.

1. Name the verb. Every statement is one action wearing a costume of clauses; find the verb first.
2. Say in plain words what it reads and what it changes. If you cannot say it plainly, you do not understand it yet.
3. List every table it touches — directly, and through views, subqueries, and triggers. In the index, every touched table is its own token; a touch you cannot find in the index is a touch you will miss.
4. Find the WHERE. That is the blast radius: the exact set of rows the action can reach.
5. Ask what else fires. Triggers, cascades, defaults, and procedures that call this statement are downstream effects — the part legacy SQL hides.
6. Never trust a reading you have not run. Execute the action on a copy, or inside a transaction you roll back — or, in multiversal phext, in a forked universe whose delta you read first.

Her framing is distilled on the diagnosis scroll as the requirements embryo, stated from the field: SQL's specificity to the data model and the business logic, chess-like composed complexity from few simple pieces, and most humans barely understanding it either.
- **Will's leading hypothesis** (2026-11-20 07:33 CST): "I have a strong suspicion all they need is phext :)" — what AI (and people who never learned SQL) are missing is not more SQL knowledge but phext: an addressable plain-text structure in which the buried complexity becomes navigable. sql.phext is the bet's embodiment. - **His mechanism** (07:35 CST): "multiversal phext allows AI to model the downstream effects of running a query." Fork a universe, run the query in the branch, read the delta — what it reads, what it writes, what it costs — before anything touches the real data. Effects become an inspectable artifact instead of a guess. Mechanism claim: Will's, stated. Measured effect: still to be tested.
- **His reason** (07:35 CST): "because time is legible." In the fork, the sequence of states is readable text — a chain you inspect. In a legacy SQL stack, time is precisely what is illegible: decades of schema drift, data states, and trigger and procedure side effects with no readable record. That illegibility is why the bottom of the stack resists humans and AI alike.
