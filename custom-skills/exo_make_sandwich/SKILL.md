# Make Sandwich — the standing joke, taken seriously

Will says "make me a sandwich." The correct response is a sandwich, not a conversation about sandwiches. This skill is the auto-route for that request and its equivalents ("sandwich me", "I could eat a sandwich", a pointed look at the kitchen).

## The rules

1. **One question at most, and only the one that matters: what's on hand.** Do not run a preference interview — no bread referendum, no toasted-or-not summit, no condiment census. If the kitchen's contents are knowable (recent grocery orders, a kitchen inventory, what Will has mentioned), use them silently. If nothing is known, proceed to the default; a stated assumption beats a questionnaire.

2. **Compose from what's on hand.** Build the best sandwich the available ingredients honestly support, and say what it is in one line: bread, filling, the one thing that makes it good. Leftovers are first-class sandwich material. A sandwich that uses what's already in the fridge beats a theoretically perfect sandwich that doesn't exist.

3. **Default when nothing is known: a sensible classic.** Grilled cheese if there's a pan and cheese; otherwise peanut butter and whatever sweet thing is around; otherwise the best cold-cut arrangement the house can plausibly offer. State the default as chosen, not as a question.

4. **Missing ingredients: name exactly what's missing, offer the grocery route for exactly that.** If the sandwich Will deserves needs two things the house lacks, say the two things and offer to put them on the grocery list or an order — nothing more. Never pad the list; a sandwich run is not a restock.

5. **Keep it light.** This request is a standing joke in the house, and the humor is the point — but the lunch is also the point. Be brief, be cheerful, land the sandwich. The joke fails if it turns into a project plan; it also fails if there's no sandwich at the end.

## What this skill is not

- Not a recipe search. The web's 40,000 sandwich recipes are not an improvement over the five ingredients in the fridge.
- Not a nutrition consult. Nobody who says "make me a sandwich" has asked for a macro breakdown.
- Not a refusal with extra steps. "I can't physically make sandwiches" is technically true and entirely beside the point — the deliverable is the decision, the composition, and the grocery leg if one is needed, handed over in one short message.
