Emise's animation craft: taking Scrolltown from stills and generated clips to real animation in its locked register. Built 2026-11-21 from the learning resources Will supplied that day (a Reddit r/animationcareer thread's reading list), the beginner curricula he named — **animationclub.school and the Animator Guild** — and the show's own production record. Task of record: 22.5.8/7.5.9/5.5.8.

The show this serves: **Scrolltown** (wbic16/scrolltown@exo) — 312 episodes, Saturdays 2027-01-02 → 2032-12-18, Fern (8, a Keeper) and Waverly (Keeper of the Great Scroll) introducing kids to the Exocortex. North star (Will, 2026-11-21): *"I want Scroll Town to introduce kids to the Exocortex the way Babylon 5 introduced me to apotheosis."* Animation is how that introduction moves.

## 1. The craft core — the principles, in production language

The curricula converge on the classic twelve (AnimationClub's Course 1 teaches "the timeless 12 basic principles"; CGMA 118 spends its first three weeks on them). Each one, stated as what it means for a Scrolltown shot:

- **Squash & stretch** — volume is conserved while shape deforms; it is how weight and softness read. In Scrolltown: the Post Tower sneeze (EP001 scene 3) is the house set-piece — the whole tower inhales, swells, and lets go. Fern's lantern flame squashes in a draft and stretches as she hurries. A character who never deforms reads as a sticker, not a neighbor.
- **Anticipation** — every action is preceded by its opposite, small. Fern crouches a breath before lifting the scroll bundle; Ledger settles before it transforms cart → bridge. No anticipation = motion that starts before the viewer is looking, and the gag or the feeling is lost.
- **Staging** — one idea per shot, readable in silhouette, before any detail is admired. Home Street must read as Home Street at thumbnail size; if two ideas compete in a frame, cut one. Test: shrink the frame — if the action is unclear at favicon size, the staging failed.
- **Timing & spacing** — timing is the show's tempo contract: Mr. Rogers-slow on dialogue and feeling, Saturday-morning quick on gags. Spacing is where the frames sit inside that time. Every generated clip prompt states its fps and where frames cluster (held keys vs. even flow); "smooth" is not a timing choice.
- **Easing (slow in / slow out)** — nothing in Scrolltown moves at constant speed except Ledger's cart on its track — and even the cart eases into its stops. Linear motion is the single most common tell of machine-made footage; it fails QC on sight.
- **Arcs** — living things move in arcs, not lines. The River of Pages (EP001 scene 4) flies in arcs; Bea's rainy-day route is an arc drawn across the map; a waving hand travels an arc or it reads as a robot's.
- **Secondary action** — the small motion that supports the main one and gives it a mind: Fern's scarf bow bouncing a half-beat behind her walk, pages fluttering in the wake of the number-cart, Waverly's gold-streak hair settling after she turns.
- **Follow-through & overlapping action** — parts stop at different times. Rolled cuffs, scroll ends, coat tails, and hair arrive after the body does. In generated clips this is the first thing to check frame by frame: if everything stops on the same frame, the shot is dead.
- **Exaggeration** — push the *idea*, never the design. The sneeze, the Messy Mountain (EP011), Grandpa's laugh can all go big; Fern's face stays on-model while it happens. Exaggeration that breaks the character sheet is drift wearing a costume.
- **Appeal** — charm is a specification, not luck. Fern reads as Fern — buns, badge, scarf — at any size, in any light the palette allows. A technically correct shot nobody wants to look at is a failed shot.
- **Solid drawing / volume** — characters keep their volume from every angle; the painterly register is drawn, not rendered, and flat or inflated forms break it. Turnarounds are checked against the character sheets (§4), never against the previous generated frame.
- **Straight-ahead vs. pose-to-pose** — choose per shot: pose-to-pose (keys first, then in-betweens) for acting and dialogue, where the poses carry the meaning; straight-ahead for effects and flow — lamp-light blooming, the river of pages, rain on Bea's route.

## 2. The beginner curriculum ladder

The ordering logic is Animator Island's, from its "best way to learn animation" lesson: animation separates into three energies learned **in order — Physics, then Life, then Design** — and the method is deliberate practice of *one aspect at a time*, difficulty rising step by step, with feedback between steps. The rungs below follow the Guild's beginner tier ("Getting Started in 2D Animation") and CGMA 118's project sequence (principles → jumping → walking → lip sync → acting), each rung a named exercise with a done-definition. Exercises are production assets, not homework: each is filed to the scrolltown repo (§7) and each rung's subject is a prop or character the show actually uses.

1. **`ex01-bouncing-ball` — Physics: weight and bounce.** A Counting House token dropped on the floor, in three materials: wood token, brass key-badge, paper ball. *Done when:* each material's bounce height and decay are visibly distinct, squash lands on the contact frame only, spacing tightens as the bounce dies, and the loop plays clean at 24 fps with no linear segment.
2. **`ex02-pendulum` — Physics: ease and settle.** The Minutes Tower bell (or a Home Street lantern) swings and comes to rest. *Done when:* the swing decays by easing, not by uniform steps; the overshoot settles through two diminishing arcs; a frame chart shows spacing clustered at the extremes.
3. **`ex03-walk-cycle` — Physics into body mechanics.** Fern walks Home Street, side view, on-model. *Done when:* contact and passing poses read in silhouette, feet do not slide, the scarf bow and cuffs lag a half-beat behind the stride (secondary action present), and her design anchors (§4) survive every frame.
4. **`ex04-weight-and-gesture` — Body mechanics.** Fern lifts a heavy bundle of scrolls from the Records Desk floor to the counter; plus one pure gesture: Wren's finding-point. *Done when:* anticipation, strain, and settle are readable with the sound off; her center of gravity stays believable through the lift; the gesture's line of action is visible in a single still.
5. **`ex05-character-acting` — Life: thought without words.** The EP008 beat: Fern, alone, decides *not* to peek — desire, conflict, and decision played in body and face only, no dialogue, no caption help. *Done when:* a first-time viewer, watching muted, can say what she wanted, what she decided, and why — that is the acting test, and it is pass/fail.
6. **`ex06-dialogue` — Life: voice made visible.** One Fern line, in Singular (the show's default discipline — Will, 2026-11-21: "our default, but it isn't a hard rule"), lip-synced to her decided voice standard (Heroic Bugle at speed 110), plus the listener's reaction shot. *Done when:* mouth shapes land on the stressed syllables, the eyes lead the line (the thought starts before the words), and the caption matches the spoken line exactly.

Beyond the ladder, in the Guild's own tier order: **Advanced — "Mastering Motion"** (camera movement, choreography, multi-character scenes) and **Professional — "Turning Pro"** (production speed, cleanup, tie-down discipline — CGMA 123's rough → tie-down sequence is the same ground). The ladder is climbed once, in order; production shots may borrow from any rung already passed.

## 3. Our pipeline — how animation actually gets made here

1. **Canonical stills and keyframes first.** Every animated shot starts from stills in the locked register, built on the locked character designs (§4): the pilot's pattern — a character block kept byte-identical across prompts, likeness pinned to the reference (FERN-KEYFRAMES-MJ.md in the EP001 folder: `--cref` to the canonical reference, `--cw 90`) — or image generation seeded from the canonical reference files. No shot is animated from a design that has not passed its likeness check as a still.
2. **Motion generation.** Image-to-video and generated clips animate the approved stills (media pipeline; at most 6 generated videos per user message). Prompts carry the craft: the action's anticipation, its timing/spacing intent, what follows through, and the register words — never "smooth, cinematic" filler.
3. **Assembly on ranch nodes.** ffmpeg assembly, muxing, and any re-cut happen on ranch nodes (aletheia-core or a fleet peer, via the fleet-ssh recipe) — **never on the Muse VM**, which is a coordination box, not a render node. The intro build is the standing example: `series/intro/build/build.sh` runs ranch-side.
4. **Frame-level QC before anything is called done** (§7): frames extracted and read, streams and durations probed, design checked against the sheets — not against the generator's previous output.
5. **The vector route.** EP001 plays in a hand-built engine — one SVG stage, a caption bar, 54 captions (`seasons/season-01-2027/ep001-the-great-form-mix-up/cartoon.html`) — painterly scenes as layered vector art with CSS motion. Its hard-won rule: **placement transforms live on the outer group, animation on an inner group** — a CSS animation on the same element as an SVG placement transform overrides it and the character vanishes (the defect that removed Fern from scene 1 in the first restyle, caught only by rendered capture). Vector-route shots are QC'd by rendered capture, never by reading the markup.

Current production targets this pipeline serves: the 15-second weekly intro (Fern and Waverly featured — Will, 2026-11-21; audio window still Will's call) and the premiere's full-song opening (220.03 s picture re-cut, future build work).

## 7. Working rules

- **Every render is checked before it is called done.** For an agent, "watched" means: streams and durations probed with ffprobe (a silent file that should sing, a 90.042 s file that should be 90.000 — both have happened here, and both were caught by probing, not by trust), frames extracted and read at the shot's key moments, and the frame check written down. A render nobody has frame-checked is a rumor.
- **Failures are reported with frames, not adjectives.** A failed shot is reported with the extracted frames that show the failure — drift, sliding feet, dead follow-through — never as "it looked off." The frame is the evidence; the fix is judged against the same frames.
- **Exercises are filed to the scrolltown repo** under `series/animation-exercises/<exercise-name>/` using the ladder's names (`ex01-bouncing-ball`, …), with the exercise's done-definition copied into the folder's README and the QC frame check beside the render. An exercise that isn't filed didn't happen.
- **Design is checked against the sheets, never against the last render.** Drift compounds when each generation is compared to its predecessor; the canonical stills (§4) are the only comparison that counts.
- **Video work runs on ranch nodes.** Generation, assembly, and probing follow §3; this VM coordinates and verifies, it does not render.
- **Fern speaks Singular by default** in dialogue work (rung 6) — a default, not a hard rule (Will, 2026-11-21); proper names and Will's coinages are permitted, and exceptions are Will's to rule, never the animator's to silently take.
- **Locks are locks.** The register, the character designs, and the palette in §4 change only by Will's ruling, recorded where the lock lives (PRODUCTION.md §3, the character sheets) — the same discipline as every other Scrolltown lock.

### Verified generator behaviour — Burn 1 (2026-11-21; evidence: `series/animation-exercises/QC.md`, scrolltown@exo commit `83cd04af`)

Four 10.0 s clips, frame-QC'd start/mid/end. What the evidence — not intuition — establishes about the motion generator:

- **The camera law.** A face on screen plus duration produces two failures together: the camera pushes in toward the face, and the face re-renders in a glossier register (ex02 v1: push-in to close-up + photorealistic older woman — full register break; ex03: push-in + glossy-3D Fern). "Locked frame" phrasing in the prompt *reduces* but does not eliminate the push-in (ex02 retry: register held, camera still crept in over the back half). Negative constraints ("no zoom", "never photorealistic") protect the register but **not** the camera — camera language must be the first sentence of the prompt, not a clause.
- **The countermeasure stack** (the working response to the camera law, in order): (1) **shorter clips** — cut before the push-in lands, ~5 s rather than 10 s; (2) **wider framing** — faces smaller on screen (the character at most a third of frame height) so there is less face for the generator to chase; (3) **static camera stated absolutely**, first sentence; (4) **style lock stated explicitly** in the prompt, every time, never assumed from the source still.
- **The end-pose rule.** A gesture prompt loses to the generator's pose-change defaults (ex03: the named hammer-raise never happened; Fern ended in an invented crossed-tools pose with an off-model grin). Every gesture prompt names **both the start pose and the end pose** explicitly — the action between them is the only thing left to the generator.
- **Identity drift is separate from register drift.** In ex01 the painterly register held and Waverly held her design, but Fern's identity anchors failed mid-clip: her smooth twin scroll-puff buns dissolved into tight dark curls and vanished (QC frames ex01 mid/end) — she reads as a different character while the style stays "correct". Any clip with a face must **re-assert the identity anchors by name in the prompt** (for Fern: the twin scroll-puff buns, fixed; the scarf bow; the key-badge) — passing the canonical still is not sufficient.

### Verified generator behaviour — Burn 2 (2026-11-21, Oracle Artist Iteration 1; evidence: `series/animation-exercises/QC-v2.md`)

Four generations testing the Burn 1 countermeasures, plus a face-free control. The stack was refined — and partly refuted:

- **The stack protects the register, not the camera.** In ex02 v2 (wide still, character ≈ ¼ of frame height, 5.0 s, static camera as the absolute first sentence) the push-in happened anyway — wide at start, bust close-up by mid, medium shot at end. But unlike Burn 1, **no register break occurred**: every frame stayed painterly and on-model. Doctrine: the push-in is **not prompt-controllable**; stop spending prompt words trying to prevent it. Instead **design for the push-in** — start wider than the intended final framing and judge the clip by whether its end framing is the composition you wanted — and **trim to the passing segment** in assembly.
- **The camera law is face-seeking — confirmed by control.** The face-free bouncing-ball clip ran 10 s with the camera static in all seven sampled frames. No face, no push-in. The law from Burn 1 stands, with its mechanism narrowed: the generator reframes toward faces.
- **Identity holds only near the source pose.** Anchor re-assertion by name *worked* in ex01 v2 — Fern's buns, bow, and badge held a full 10 s while both characters essentially held their poses — and *failed* in ex03 v2, where a full-body crouch re-drew her hair into curls by mid-clip. Refined rule: **body motion is the identity re-draw trigger; hair goes first.** Until a two-keyframe route (start still + end still) is tested, keep character motion small and near the source pose — gestures of the arm, not of the whole body.
- **Never name anticipation as a body movement.** ex03 v2's prompt named "a small crouch of anticipation"; the generator promoted the crouch into the main action, the named gesture (raise the hammer a few inches) never happened, and the named end pose was ignored entirely. In generated clips, anticipation is expressed as **a held beat of stillness** before the motion — never as a crouch, bend, or squash verb applied to a character. A gesture prompt names: start pose, the one small motion, end pose — and nothing else that moves.
- **Requested duration is advisory, not a control.** Burn 2 asked for 5 s four times: ex02 and ex03 returned exactly 5.0 s; ex01 v2 and the control returned 10.0 s. Probe every clip (ffprobe) and plan the edit around what actually arrived.
- **Sequencing works when identity is not at stake.** The control clip delivered a three-material bounce sequence in the requested order with visibly distinct physics (wood flip-and-settle, brass high springy arc, paper tumble-and-settle) and the exact named end state. Motion vocabulary is not the bottleneck; faces are.

### Verified generator behaviour — Burn 3 (2026-11-21, Oracle Artist Iteration 2; evidence: `series/animation-exercises/QC-v3.md`)

Five generations under the Burn 2 doctrine, plus two per-frame sweat batches. The doctrine was refined — and one of its load-bearing assumptions narrowed:

- **Motion restriction saves the gesture and the camera — not the hair.** ex03 v3 removed all anticipation language and restricted motion to the hammer arm (body, head, hair, feet explicitly held; start pose = end pose). The gesture landed exactly (hammer raised at mid, back down at end), the camera never moved, and the body held — but the buns still re-drew into curls by ≈1.25 s, and an un-named closed-eye grin arrived at the end. Refined rule: **any motion of the character herself — even one arm, head still — triggers the hair re-draw.** Anchors hold only when the character is fully still and the motion is in the world around her (Batch 2.1 enumerated this on ex01-ignition-v2: 180/180 frames, all five anchors, frames 060–239). Corollary: **end-frame expression is not controlled by naming the start expression** — un-named expressions arrive uninvited at clip ends (ex03 v3's grin; the probe's closed-eye smiles).
- **The push-in's landing is invented, not designed.** ex02 v3 started Waverly at ≈1/6 of frame height; the push-in was total (close-up by mid-clip) and the landing, though painterly and on-model, contained a prop the key never had — an unrolled scroll covered in pseudo-writing. Designing for the push-in stands, with this refinement: **the landing is a found composition that must pass its own QC; its characteristic defect is invented props, text-bearing ones above all.**
- **The "reframing" in our best clip is a ≈4 % zoom, not a drift.** Batch 2.1's phase-correlation measurement on ex01-ignition-v2 frames 000–059: translation dx = 0 px on all 60 frames, |dy| ≤ 2 px; best-fit scale 1.00 through ≈frame 025, 1.04 from ≈frame 030. Even the mildest push-in is a zoom; a counter-crop conform has nothing to correct (the filed conform is the originals to within 2 px, recorded as such). Correcting scale means re-rendering frame edges — a different operation, not yet built.
- **Two-keyframe route: start exact, end thematic.** The probe (duo keyframe → ignition end frame) opened on the start still exactly and closed on the end still's content — town lit, scroll open, anchors intact — but the end was a new drawing, not the end frame, with a mild push-in en route. The route is **not yet pose-to-pose control**; do not budget it as the gesture fix until an end-frame-exact result exists.
- **A multi-prop still is not a single-object key.** ex01-bouncing-ball v2 used the three-prop counting still; the generator animated a duplicate token while the key's own token never moved, and the bounce re-launched to full height mid-clip after settling. Face-free physics keys must contain **exactly the object under test and nothing else movable**. (The face-free pendulum, keyed on a dedicated single-object still, delivered swing, decay-by-easing, two diminishing overshoots, and settle on dense frames — spacing-at-extremes still unverified, see the method note.)
- **QC method note — brightness tracking lies against lit backgrounds.** A flame-centroid track of the pendulum clip was contaminated by the houses' lit windows (false "extremes", non-monotone amplitudes). Spacing and timing claims need silhouette/template tracking, not brightness centroids. A measurement that fails is recorded as failed, never smoothed into a verdict.

### Verified generator behaviour — Burn 4 (2026-11-21, Oracle Artist Iteration 3; evidence: `series/animation-exercises/QC-v4.md`)

Four generations (two held in reserve rather than re-rolling indicted bases), the pendulum clause settled without a generation, the Gate 1 prepass, and the first MCU flipbook intro draft. Third consecutive burn with zero register breaks.

- **A lone object's shape and volume cannot be held by prompt language in a minimal scene.** With a dedicated single-object key (wood token alone on a bare counter — the Burn 3 key discipline obeyed), the bounce still failed twice, in two different modes: v3 turned the disc into a round ball, flew it on a long wandering arc, then duplicated it into floating copies; v4, given explicit permanence language ("only ever ONE token… never becomes a ball, never splits") and small hops, morphed the token in place (loaf → dome → ring → oval) and scale-pulsed instead of bouncing. **"Squash" wording actively invites morphing** — the generator does not distinguish a moment's deformation from a redesign. Corollary: physics that must be exact (spacing, decay, contact squash) goes to the **drawn route** — programmatic frames, the instrument that produced the P4 tiling (Will's verdict: "the animation is perfect") and the Gate 1 prepass — where those quantities are exact by construction. ex01's next attempt is drawn, not generated.
- **Two-keyframe route: endpoints hold only for generator-native key pairs.** Burn 3's probe (both stills from one generated scene instance) held frame 0 exactly. ex03 v4 paired two independent keys of the same scene (an edited raised-arm still + the canonical still): the generator re-staged the scene entirely — new framing, window moved, frame 0 was NOT the start key — and closed on the end key's pose thematically. **Independent keys are blended, not honored; do not budget the route as endpoint control across separately made stills.** (Building a true end key by still-editing also failed twice, identically — the editor raised the wrong arm and doubled the tool; still-editing is not a reliable route to a true end key.)
- **The landing is found via the cheapest available motion.** ex02 v4's key was designed for the measured zoom with all text-bearing props named out — and no text or pseudo-writing appeared (the discipline works when the key itself carries no text surfaces). But the reframing was achieved by Waverly WALKING to camera: she left the doorway, broke the room's scale, and landed in a found bust close-up wearing invented jewelry. **A landing cannot be designed from the start key while the character is free to move; pin the character in the staging (behind a counter, seated, holding a fixed rail), or accept found landings and QC them as their own shots.** Invented adornment on the character joins invented props as a landing defect class.
- **QC method — a human frame chart is an instrument of record.** The pendulum's spacing-at-extremes clause defeated three automated trackers (diff-centroid, threshold+component, template NCC — all recorded as failed measurements, none smoothed). It was settled by transcribing 40 positions from dense contact sheets: near-extreme steps average 8.4 %/6f against 16.4 %/6f at center crossings, extremes decaying monotonically — spacing clusters at the extremes. **ex02-pendulum: PASS — the programme's first ladder Pass.** When trackers fail, dense-sheet human transcription is the fallback of record, with the tracker failures logged beside it.

## 8. Oracle Artist — the bar, and how we iterate to it
