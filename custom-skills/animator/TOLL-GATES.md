## 1. The rule

The road to Oracle Artist is gated by reproduction. A toll gate is passed only by **REPRODUCING** its film through our pipeline:

1. **Prepass** (artist-dispatch): an arc sketch derived from the original's actual shots — low-fidelity reference points, the shared guiding light — then a fast full-sequence prepass against that sketch.
2. **Full render**: the production pass at the locked register.
3. **QC by frame comparison**: the reproduction is compared against the original at matched timestamps — composition, timing, motion beats — and the verdict is recorded in `ITERATIONS.md`.

Gates are taken **in order**. No gate is skipped. A gate's reproduction is a training/study work: the point is the craft the film teaches, measured against the film itself.

## 3. Legal basis and caveats

- **Basis.** US public-domain status per the cited Wikipedia list and its underlying sources — principally *Film Superlist: Motion Pictures in the U.S. Public Domain* (Walter E. Hurst), the Catalog of Copyright Entries, the Walter Lantz Cartune Encyclopedia's list of shorts in the public domain, and Cartoon Research. Most enumerated entries are public domain because their copyright was never renewed; the U.S. Government sections are public domain as works of the United States government; pre-1931 films are public domain by age.
- **Separate-elements caveat** (the source page's own caveat, recorded): while a film in and of itself may be in the public domain, the original versions may incorporate elements that remain under a separate copyright. Music is the standing example — see the † notes in the canon (†a–†c: original film only, Blue Ribbon reissue copyrighted and renewed; †d: visuals only, music copyrighted and renewed).
- **Trademarks.** Character trademarks are a separate matter from film copyright and govern any public or commercial use of character likenesses. Our gate reproductions are study works; any later publication of a reproduction gets its own review before it ships.

## 4. Gate setup

Each gate, when its turn comes:

1. Source a reference copy of the original from a public archive (e.g. archive.org), fetched at human pace — the good-netizen rule: human-scale rates; a site's stated limits, 429s, and bot walls are respected as final.
2. File the reference copy ranch-side (video work lives on the ranch, never on this VM).
3. Derive the arc sketch from the original's actual shots (artist-dispatch Step 0), then run the gate per §1.

## 6. Transcription notes

- **Order and groupings are the page's own** (Warner Bros. → MGM → Universal → Paramount, incl. Fleischer, Famous, Popeye, Superman, George Pal → Celebrity Productions → Van Beuren → Ted Eshbaugh → Walt Disney Productions → Terrytoons → Columbia → U.S. Government films). Titles and years are transcribed as listed, including the films' own period spellings (e.g. *The Shanty Where Santy Claus Lives*, *The Stupidstitious Cat*, *Confusions of a Nutzy Spy*).
- **Series-level notes are notes, not gates.** Where the page asserts a whole class without enumerating titles, no gates were created:
  - Warner Bros. / Paramount / Walt Disney / Terrytoons headers: every short from 1930 (and before) is in the public domain as of 2026 — a class assertion; the page enumerates no individual 1930 titles, so no gates were created for them.
  - Oswald the Lucky Rabbit: all Winkler Pictures films and all 1930 films are stated to be in the public domain — class assertion, not enumerated; not gates.
  - Noveltoons: 'almost all Noveltoons before 1950' are stated to be unrenewed and public domain, except Cilly Goose and Gabriel Churchkitten (both 1944). Only the page's enumerated titles are gates; the two named exceptions are not public domain and are not gates.
  - U.S. Government films — 'Other government films': all 1943–1944 Fred Brunish stop-motion instructional films for the U.S. Navy — a series-level entry with no individual titles on the page; recorded as a note, not a gate.
  - Miscellaneous (page's final section): all U.S.-based productions made before 1931, including much of the Felix the Cat, Out of the Inkwell, Krazy Kat, Aesop's Fables, Winsor McCay and Laugh-O-Gram Studio libraries — a blanket class assertion; not gates.
- **Private Snafu appears in two places on the page by design**: the Warner Bros. government-films section declines to list the Snafu films there (produced directly by the US Government); they are enumerated — and gated — under U.S. Government films (Gates 488–513).
- **Superman numbering glitch**: the page numbers the Superman list 1, 1, 2 … 16; the section holds 17 distinct films, transcribed in page order as Gates 361–377.
- **† notes from the page**: †a *Flop Goes the Weasel*, †b *Fin 'n' Catty*, †c *An Itch in Time* — original film only; the Blue Ribbon reissue is copyrighted and renewed. †d *Susie the Little Blue Coupe* — visuals only; the music is copyrighted and renewed.
- **Years**: every entry carries the year the page gives it, except Private Snafu, where the page gives no year per title (series produced 1943–1945) — marked —.
- **The Warner Bros. government-films entry *Bugs Bunny* (1942)** is transcribed under the title the page gives it.
